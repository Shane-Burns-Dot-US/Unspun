# Fête Kids — Experience-to-Atomic Process Atlas (v1)

**Method:** Backward chaining from the customer wish. We construct the raving review first; treat each sentence of that review as a *received outcome*; trace each outcome to the operational moment that produced it; group those moments at five layers of abstraction; re-cut the same atoms under six different lenses (for indexing iterating designs); then score each atom for AI/agentic automatability against the benchmark quality threshold.

**Working team voice:**
- *Consultant (C):* MECE structure, layering, evaluative framing.
- *Brand Manager (BM):* Promise integrity, voice, what is non-negotiable.
- *Designer (D):* Craft, taste, sensory and aesthetic moments, perceived continuity.
- *Developer (Dev):* What is tractable to model, where probabilistic systems hit the benchmark, where they don't.

**Scope discipline:** *What* and *why*, not *when* or *how-to-build*. No time estimates. No staffing. No sequencing. This atlas is the spine we iterate designs against.

## 4. Variant Groupings (re-cuts of the same atoms — for design iteration)

The atoms are stable. The *groupings* are the lenses we'll iterate against. Each lens reveals a different design constraint.

**G1 — Stage vs Back-of-house.** Did the customer perceive this? If yes, it must be rendered to her aesthetic; if no, it must be ruthlessly efficient. The boundary is the membrane in R15.

**G2 — Taste-bearing vs Logistics-bearing.** Taste atoms cannot be a benchmark miss; logistics atoms can be (and recover). Different quality bars, different automation thresholds.

**G3 — Reversible vs Brand-destroying.** A late floral substitution is reversible. A bad photo of the host's mother-in-law in the shared album is not. Drives risk weighting in automation scoring.

**G4 — Information artifact axis.** Group by what's produced: *the brief*, *the moodboard*, *the vendor pack*, *the contract bundle*, *the run-of-show*, *the cue sheet*, *the album*, *the recap doc*. Each artifact is a unit of iteration.

**G5 — Asset compounding axis.** Group by which of the two compounding assets each atom builds: the **referral asset** (customer loop) or the **vendor network asset** (supply loop). Atoms that build neither get scrutinized.

**G6 — Sensory channel axis.** Group by which channel the customer received it through: written, visual, spoken, kinesthetic (room flow), social (other guests' reaction). Used by D to audit completeness — most parties under-invest in spoken and social.

**G7 — Cost-driver axis.** Group by what makes this expensive today: planner hours, vendor margin, error tax, opportunity cost. Drives the automation *value* score.

## 6. AI / Agentic Automation Assessment

**Scoring axes:**
- **P(auto):** Probability an agent can hit the benchmark at near-CD-grade quality, *given current frontier capabilities and a well-built workflow*. H ≥ 0.75, M 0.4–0.75, L < 0.4.
- **Value:** Leverage on planner hours, margin, error cost, or moat. H = high leverage on the 3x throughput thesis; M = meaningful; L = marginal.
- **Quadrant:**
  - **AUTO** — High P, High/Med Value → automate now, agent is the operator.
  - **AUG-HITL** — Med P, High Value → agent drafts, human approves; the leverage is real but the brand risk needs a checkpoint.
  - **HUMAN-AI** — Low/Med P, High Value, taste-bearing or relationship-bearing → human leads, agent provides scaffolding, tooling, recall, and zero-effort prep.
  - **HUMAN** — Low P or brand-destroying if missed → human-only; agent stays out of the customer-perceived loop.

## 7. Quadrant Summary & Strategic Read

### Quadrant counts

| Quadrant | Count | % | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AUTO** | 22 | 28% | Backbone of the 3x throughput. Mostly back-of-house: enrichment, contracting, ledger, vendor portal, content pipeline, dossier and vendor-graph updates. This is where the platform leverage compounds. |
| **AUG-HITL** | 28 | 36% | The "agent drafts, human approves" middle. Largest single bucket — and the right place to focus iteration, because P(auto) here is improvable and Value is high. The brand-risk checkpoint stays cheap. |
| **HUMAN-AI** | 13 | 17% | Live physical / relational work where the agent's job is *to make the human's job effortless*: scaffolding, recall, decision trees, prep, countdowns. Tooling-driven leverage. |
| **HUMAN** | 15 | 19% | The irreducible brand core: CD taste arbitration, the named human voice, the live read of the room, the handwritten note. These are the moments customers pay for. |

### What this tells us

**1. The 3x is real, but it doesn't come from automating the front of house.** It comes from AUTO + AUG-HITL = 64% of atomic tasks, almost all back-of-house and pipeline-grade. The throughput math is dominated by enrichment, vendor matching, contracts, ledger, photo pipeline, and the compounding data assets (vendor graph T71, customer dossier T73). These are where agents replace planner hours one-for-one and then some.

**2. The brand lives in 15 atoms.** The HUMAN quadrant is the irreducible spine of the customer's perception: **T08, T10, T14, T19, T20, T26, T45, T51, T54, T56, T60, T62, T68, T78** plus T06. Every one of these is in the *Stage* half of G1. If any of them degrades, the review collapses — because each one corresponds to a load-bearing sentence in the review (R3, R5, R6, R7, R8, R10, R11, R13, R14, R15). The strategic implication: **the planner's day, post-automation, should be these 15 atoms and nothing else.**

**3. AUG-HITL is the design surface that matters most.** 28 atoms, all with high value and improvable P(auto). Iterating workflows here moves the throughput frontier and protects brand integrity simultaneously. The lens to use during iteration: G3 (reversible vs brand-destroying) decides where the human checkpoint stays even after P(auto) climbs.

**4. The compounding assets are AUTO.** T71 (vendor scoring), T72 (vendor onboarding into the gated tier — AUG-HITL), T73 (customer dossier), T75 (referral attribution). The flywheel runs on agents, not humans. Every event makes the next event easier. This is the platform thesis materially showing up at the atomic layer.

**5. The "membrane" (G1 boundary) is the most important brand asset.** Front-of-house atoms are scored against *aesthetic and emotional* benchmarks; back-of-house atoms are scored against *speed and accuracy*. Iterating designs must respect this division — a back-of-house atom that leaks to the customer becomes a stage atom and must be re-rendered.

### Variant-grouping cross-reads (for design iteration)

- **G2 (Taste vs Logistics):** Taste atoms cluster in HUMAN and HUMAN-AI; logistics atoms cluster in AUTO and AUG-HITL. This is the strongest predictor of the right quadrant.
- **G3 (Brand-destroying risk):** The 15 HUMAN atoms are 80% of the brand-destroying-risk surface. The other 20% sits in 5 AUG-HITL atoms with elevated checkpointing needs (T04, T05, T13, T46, T58).
- **G4 (Information artifacts):** The brief, the moodboard, the proposal, the ROS, the cue sheet, the album, the recap. Iteration cycle should be artifact-by-artifact, since each one is the unit the customer or staff actually interacts with.
- **G5 (Asset compounding):** ~14 atoms build the referral asset; ~11 build the vendor network asset; the rest are single-event. Single-event atoms with low compounding return are the candidates for ruthless agent automation, since they don't build moat — they pay throughput.

### What we are *not* doing in this document

No build sequencing, no time scoping, no staffing plan, no roadmap. The atlas is the index. The iteration of design happens against it — each iteration takes a grouping (a G-lens, or a quadrant, or a phase), proposes a workflow, and is evaluated against the benchmark column and the failure-mode column of the affected atoms.

## Appendix B — One-line definitions

- **Benchmark quality threshold:** "5-star looks like…" column on each atom. Agents must hit this in expectation, not just on average.
- **HUMAN quadrant:** Not "AI can't help here." It means: the customer-facing moment is human, and the agent's role is invisible support to that human.
- **AUG-HITL:** Agent does the work, human owns the brand outcome. The checkpoint is the brand's insurance policy.
- **The membrane:** The line in G1 between what the customer perceives and what she does not. Crossing it un-rendered breaks R15.
